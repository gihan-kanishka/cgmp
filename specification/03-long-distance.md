# Long-Distance and Displacement Policy - v1.1

## Principle

CGMP guarantees a fair outbound fare under published rules. It does not guarantee a profitable round trip.

Ordinary distance and time charges compensate the outbound trip. A separate provision may be added only for a defined, necessary displacement cost caused by the trip.

## Potentially eligible costs

Depending on market and route:

- accommodation where immediate safe return is unreasonable;
- a defined meal allowance associated with such a stay;
- unavoidable tolls or route-specific fees;
- another published displacement category.

## Not automatically compensated

CGMP does not automatically add:

- full empty-return vehicle cost;
- hypothetical lost future wages;
- guaranteed round-trip profit;
- discretionary after-the-fact driver claims.

## Pre-trip calculation and consent

Any displacement provision must be determined before trip acceptance from published rules, based on known or estimable route/departure/ETA/vehicle factors, shown to the passenger, and accepted before dispatch.

## Relationship to expanded search

Long-distance trips still follow the normal dispatch sequence:

1. deterministic nearby search;
2. passenger-authorized deterministic expanded search in 2 km increments;
3. sealed fallback only if those authorized deterministic searches fail.

A long-distance provision is part of the deterministic baseline before any sealed bid is considered.

## Safe-return decision

A deployment may compare reasonable same-day return conditions with a stay provision, but safety and feasibility override a purely monetary comparison.

## Route overrides

Markets may define documented route-severity overrides for destinations where terrain, weather, road conditions or vehicle restrictions make ordinary assumptions inappropriate.

The override methodology must be published and auditable.
